Research

I am broadly interested in computational complexity theory and value conceptual and creative ideas. Currently, I am mostly focused on the following areas:

Fine-grained complexity

Proving non-trivial lower bounds in strong computational models (like RAM-machine) is generally extremely hard. Let's instead prove conditional lower bounds: assuming a widely believed conjectures (as OV or APSP) establish that some other problems cannot be solved faster than n^2 or n^3.

Structural complexity

While in FGC we focus on individual algorithms, in structural complexity we study large complexity classes and connections between them. Sometimes brilliant results emerge: for example, polynomially bounded interactive protocols (IP) can unexpectedly compute all of PSPACE, and a single counting oracle (#P) is enough to capture the entire polynomial hierarchy (PH).

Non-uniform complexity

Non-uniform circuits (Boolean or algebraic) are extremely powerful: they can recognize undecidable languages. While proving circuit size lower bounds for natural problems is notoriously difficult, they surprisingly appear as barriers to many unresolved questions. For example, PIT derandomization, as well as SETH-hardness of 3SUM, implies strong circuit lower bounds for Boolean or algebraic circuits.
